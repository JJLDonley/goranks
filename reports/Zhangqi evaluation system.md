# Zhangqi turns KataGo reviews into Fox ranks

涨棋网 (Zhangqi, zhangqi.com.cn) is a small Beijing cloud-review service, run by 北京奕齐伟业科技有限责任公司, that sells KataGo reviews and puts each game on its own **0–12 "棋力" scale, where below 10 one unit is one Fox (野狐) rank band**. The interesting parts of its evaluation can be read in its public front-end code. 吻合度 (match rate), 平均目差 (average point loss), 难度 (difficulty), 发挥水准 (performance level) and the 问题手 (problem-move) list are simple, transparent rules computed in the browser from KataGo candidate lists. The strength number itself, the 高光时刻 (highlights) and the 疑似AI (suspected-AI) spans come from an undocumented server model that the site calls a "大模型" (large model). The calibration to Fox ranks is only implied, mainly by a table of 31 AI opponents built from KataGo human-SL profiles. Zhangqi publishes no validation against real Fox ranks. Its only large sample is its own: Fox-certified amateur 6d players averaged **9.6 ± 0.8** and professionals **10.1 ± 0.7**, so one game cannot tell them apart. Public reception is thin, mostly sceptical, and centred on AI-cheating disputes rather than rank accuracy. For GoRanks, Zhangqi is mainly a product benchmark. It confirms several choices GoRanks has already made: difficulty from human-SL policy, phase splits, excluding handicap and AI-like games, and aggregating over many games. It also shows what an open estimator can do that Zhangqi does not: publish its calibration and error, model analysis depth and era explicitly, and replace a weighted 30-day mean with a proper multi-game estimate that carries an uncertainty.

**Sources and method.** No method write-up exists: no help article, Zhihu post or forum explanation was found. Most facts below were read from the site's public JavaScript bundles (Vue/Vite, fetched anonymously on 2026-11-25, no login, no API calls). Chunk names carry content hashes and will change when the site redeploys. In this report, **"documented"** means stated by Zhangqi in its UI text, its code, its store listings or its own articles. **"Inference"** marks my reading of that evidence. Social-media items were often readable only as search-engine snippets, and they are flagged as such.

Bulk review cards are discounted. Around the review sit rated placement games against **31 persona bots** ("最近6局赢3局即可定级", win 3 of your last 6 to earn a rank certificate), a paid challenge against the ×30 bot 哮天犬, human game rooms whose promotion and relegation skip games against opponents with "abnormal" scores, life-and-death problems, problems built from your own blunders, lectures and academy tools ([FightView](https://www.zhangqi.com.cn/assets/FightView-Cy_YVpdq.js), [BoardRoomView](https://www.zhangqi.com.cn/assets/BoardRoomView-D0eWZntd.js), [main bundle](https://www.zhangqi.com.cn/assets/index-lbwnd-ho.js)). *Inference:* the product centres on rank estimation plus AI-cheating detection. The store copy leads with "评测对局双方本盘发挥出几段的水平，自动识别开狗作弊并预警" (rates the dan level each side played at and flags AI cheating) ([应用宝](https://sj.qq.com/appdetail/com.chb2.zhangqi)). The user base looks small, but web and mini-program users are not counted in store figures.

**Conflict in the notes, resolved.** One research pass found "no evidence of a human-SL network" in the client. Another found that the 难度 computation reads a per-candidate `human` policy value, that the analysis table has "人类偏好" (human preference) and "等级偏好" (rank-level preference) columns, and that the bot ids are exactly KataGo's human-SL profile names (`rank_15k` … `rank_9d`) ([white-stone](https://www.zhangqi.com.cn/assets/white-stone-B0RyI3Pf.js), [KataAnswers](https://www.zhangqi.com.cn/assets/KataAnswers-BgSqJB_H.js), [fightUtils](https://www.zhangqi.com.cn/assets/fightUtils-Dt7XdNFj.js)). The more specific code evidence wins: *Zhangqi almost certainly runs KataGo's human-SL network alongside the main net.* Its own tooltips do not agree on which profile, though. The "人类偏好" tooltip says "professional", while the 难度 tooltip says "top amateur".

## Six move levels drive everything except the strength number

**Documented: move classification.** Every candidate is compared with the engine's top move by Δscore (point difference) and Δwr (winrate difference) in `getAnswerLevel` ([aiUtils](https://www.zhangqi.com.cn/assets/aiUtils-D3PT-M2P.js)):

| Level | Name | Rule (vs. top candidate) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 最佳 (best) | the top candidate itself |
| 2 | 好手 (good) | Δscore ≤ 2 **and** Δwr ≤ 5 % **and** visit share > 5 % |
| 3 | 一般 (ordinary) | same loss limits as level 2, but visit share ≤ 5 % |
| 4 | 欠佳 (inaccuracy) | Δscore ≤ 6 and Δwr ≤ 15 % (beyond level 2/3) |
| 5 | 不好 (bad) | Δscore ≤ 10 and Δwr ≤ 30 % |
| 6 | 恶手 (blunder) | anything else |

When the played move is not among the candidates, the client builds an entry from the next position's evaluation, with zero visits, and classifies it by the same rule ([white-stone](https://www.zhangqi.com.cn/assets/white-stone-B0RyI3Pf.js)). Everything shown to the user, except the server outputs, is derived from these levels.

**吻合度 is two hit rates, not a top-N match.** The tooltip defines it as "AI一选或AI推荐好手的命中率" (the hit rate of the AI's first choice or AI-recommended good moves). The code counts, per colour, the share of moves at level 1 (**一选命中率**, first-choice rate) and at level ≤ 2 (**好手命中率**, good-move rate), averaged over all of that colour's moves except the last analysed position ([WinrateChart](https://www.zhangqi.com.cn/assets/WinrateChart-CODLOGXq.js), [white-stone](https://www.zhangqi.com.cn/assets/white-stone-B0RyI3Pf.js)). *Inference:* the visit-share condition is a quirk. A move that loses just as little as a 好手 but received ≤ 5 % of the visits is "一般" and is not a hit. The good-move rate therefore depends on search behaviour and visit count, not only on move quality.

**平均目差** is the mean per-move drop in the best scoreLead from the mover's side, clipped at 0. The code also keeps the standard deviation and the winrate version. **发挥水准** is simply the histogram of the player's moves over the six levels, for the whole game or for each phase (`sumSkills`). **复盘质量** (review quality) is a 0–5 star label from the minimum visits per move: <750, <2,400, <4,500, <9,000, <15,000, ≥15,000 ([fileUtils](https://www.zhangqi.com.cn/assets/fileUtils-adJOM6qz.js)).

**难度 is a human-policy error rate.** The tooltip calls it "业余顶尖棋手在本盘棋每一手下错的平均概率" (the average probability that a top amateur goes wrong on each move of this game). In code, for each move after the 4th, difficulty = 100 × (1 − Σ human-policy of candidates at level ≤ 2), and the game value is the mean over the player's moves ([white-stone](https://www.zhangqi.com.cn/assets/white-stone-B0RyI3Pf.js)). The client also computes an unused "surprise" score: the mean of `difficulty` when the player found a level-≤2 move, and `difficulty − 100` otherwise. It credits finding good moves in hard positions.

**问题手 are level ≥ 4 moves, filtered by the player's level.** `loadQuestions` takes every played move at level ≥ 4. It sorts them by level, then winrate loss, then point loss, and keeps the top N (default **10**). It can suppress "repeated blunders" (another 恶手 by the same side within 2 moves with the same AI best point) ([white-stone](https://www.zhangqi.com.cn/assets/white-stone-B0RyI3Pf.js)). A server-side filter "将屏蔽同等棋力水平的棋手极难下对的问题手" (hides problem moves that players of the same strength find extremely hard to get right), and it is available only after a 高级 (≥ 4,500 visits) review ([WinrateChart](https://www.zhangqi.com.cn/assets/WinrateChart-CODLOGXq.js)). In the PDF the level is taken from the game's own 棋力 result, or chosen by the user from "8段及以上" (8 dan and up) down to "1段以下" (below 1 dan) ([GeneratePdfModal](https://www.zhangqi.com.cn/assets/GeneratePdfModal-lxd6NkU2.js)). *Inference:* the filter almost certainly drops a mistake when the human-SL probability of a good move at the player's rank (the "等级偏好" column) falls below a threshold. The threshold is not public.

**Phases** are 布局 moves 1–60, 中盘 61–150 and 官子 151 onward. A phase score needs ≥ 20 moves, a custom range must be 40–60 moves, and the overall score is a **weighted** combination of phase scores with undisclosed weights ([WinrateChart](https://www.zhangqi.com.cn/assets/WinrateChart-CODLOGXq.js)).

## A server "large model" reads candidate lists and returns a Fox-anchored number

**Documented: input and output.** The strength request (`POST predict/rank`) sends, for every move whose analysis has **> 15 visits**, the full candidate list `[pos, scoreLead, winrate, candidateIndex]`, plus the visit counts and an optional custom range. No time data and no human-policy values are in this payload ([chartUtils](https://www.zhangqi.com.cn/assets/chartUtils-DhoSW7OM.js), [predictService](https://www.zhangqi.com.cn/assets/predictService-YLcwpNB0.js)). Per colour, the server returns a total, three phase values, `dog` spans `[start, end, similarity]` (suspected AI) and `highlight` spans `[start, end, value, text]` (高光时刻: "第s手到e手下出了X水平", from move s to move e the player played at level X). An estimate needs **≥ 100 moves** ([WinrateChart](https://www.zhangqi.com.cn/assets/WinrateChart-CODLOGXq.js)). (One note said 20 moves; that is the per-phase minimum.) The tooltip, verbatim in substance: ≥12 is "半神/AI" (demigod/AI, which very few top pros reach), 11–12 is top pro, 10–11 is pro, and **below 10 the value "对应野狐段位和等级" (corresponds to Fox dan and kyu ranks)**. It then warns that the value is "根据大模型计算…仅供参考，不能作为评级依据" (computed by a large model, for reference only, not a grading), that "棋风与AI相似的棋手会获得更高评价，AlphaGo出现之前的棋谱评价会低于实际等级" (AI-like styles score higher, and pre-AlphaGo games score below their true level), and that games played with AI help may score wrongly. The suspected-AI tooltip adds that the similarity "不能作为使用AI的证据" (is not evidence of AI use).

**Documented: the scale.** The display rule is: <0 is "1级以下" (below 1 kyu), <1 is 1 kyu, <10 is "x段" (x dan), then 职业 (pro), 一线职业 (top pro) and 半神/AI ([chartUtils](https://www.zhangqi.com.cn/assets/chartUtils-DhoSW7OM.js)). The clearest calibration evidence is the opponent table, which maps KataGo human-SL profile ids to the scale and to Fox labels ([fightUtils](https://www.zhangqi.com.cn/assets/fightUtils-Dt7XdNFj.js)):

| Bot profile | Zhangqi range | Fox label |
|---|---|---|
| `rank_10d` (Zhangqi's own id) | 12 | 超世冠 / 半神 |
| `power_10d+` / `power_10d` | 10.5–11.5 / 10.0–11.0 | top pro / pro |
| `rank_9d` | 9.5–10.5 | 业余顶尖 (top amateur) |
| `rank_8d` | 9.0–10.0 | 业余6段 (amateur 6d) |
| `rank_6d` | 8.0–9.0 | 野狐8段 (Fox 8d) |
| `rank_4d` | 7.0–8.0 | 野狐7段 (Fox 7d) |
| `rank_2d` | 6.0–7.0 | 野狐6段 (Fox 6d) |
| `rank_1k` | 5.0–6.0 | 野狐5段 (Fox 5d) |
| `rank_2k` / `rank_3k` / `rank_4k` | 4–5 / 3–4 / 2–3 | Fox 4d / 3d / 2d |
| `rank_5k` | 1.0–2.0 | 野狐1段 (Fox 1d) |
| `rank_6k` | 0–1 | 野狐1级 (Fox 1k) |
| `rank_7k` … `rank_15k` | < 0 | Fox 2k … 10k |

The odd half-steps (Fox "强N弱N+1", strong N, weak N+1) are filled by `rank_odd` profiles and custom `power_*` bots. **Inference:** the table implies Zhangqi measured its own bots, either with its own scorer or against Fox players, and found KataGo `rank_Nk` bots playing about **five to six Fox ranks above their label** at low levels. The gap narrows at the top: `rank_9d` plays at "top amateur". This is not documented. A likely explanation is that a human-SL profile combined with search plays above the human rank it imitates. Read at face value, it would also mean KataGo's KGS-2020 rank labels and Fox ranks do not line up one to one. *Inference:* the "大模型" is most likely a regression or classifier over loss, winrate and candidate-rank features, trained on modern online games labelled with Fox ranks. The admitted biases (AI style scores higher, pre-AlphaGo games score lower) are exactly what features based on engine agreement and loss would produce. Nothing public confirms the model type, the training data, the phase weights, the 高光 window or how 疑似AI is computed.

## Thirty-day weighted means, and no independent check of accuracy

**Documented: aggregation.** "当前棋力" (current strength) is "根据最近30天对局水平的均值计算" (the mean of the last 30 days' game levels). A game weighs more when its review is deeper and when it is more recent, and full review reports weigh more than plain analyses. It needs **≥ 5 assessed games**, otherwise "数据不足" (not enough data) is shown. A ± band is shown until there are **20 高级 reviews in 30 days**. Inputs are the games under linked Fox and Tencent IDs plus games against the site's bots. Handicap games of 2+ stones are excluded, as are games the user marks "不是我的对局" (not my game: AI use, or someone else on the account) ([ComputeGrow](https://www.zhangqi.com.cn/assets/ComputeGrow-YLaE1AAC.js)). A checkbox drops games scoring 12. The detail view lists games far from the current value first ([ChessGrowHelpView](https://www.zhangqi.com.cn/assets/ChessGrowHelpView-BzqdYTN8.js)). The exact recency and depth weights and the ± formula are not published. *Inference:* a plain weighted mean inside a hard 30-day window is sensitive to single outliers, and the outlier-first list is how the site hands that problem to the user.

**Documented: the only large-sample evidence is the operator's own.** Prompted by a 2026 controversy over a streamer's many "半神" games, Zhangqi reviewed 1,100 games at 15,000 visits on ten RTX 5090s. The results: ten world-champion-level pros in Fox match games averaged **11.5 ± 0.50** (41 % of games at a full 12); 20 pros in Fox ladder games **10.1 ± 0.7**; 20 Fox-certified amateur 6d players **9.6 ± 0.8**. The article itself warns that single games carry chance and that shallow analysis "可能与真相背道而驰" (can point opposite to the truth) ([SMZDM repost of Zhangqi's article](https://post.smzdm.com/p/ae6x04dq/)). *Inference:* two things follow. First, the top of the scale is compressed: pro and amateur 6d sit half a point apart with standard deviations above 0.7, so one game separates them poorly. Second, the same champions score 11.5 in match games but pros score 10.1 on the ladder, so the number measures game context (time, effort) as well as the player. Nobody has published a comparison against ordinary kyu or low-dan Fox ranks, the range most users and GoRanks care about.

**Player reports are few, sceptical and mostly snippets.** A Douyin critique ("鉴别AI的神器涨棋网，真有那么神么？", is the AI-detection wonder tool Zhangqi really that good?) concludes that the score is affected by error, winrate and opponent level and is "不够准确，不能作为绝对标准" (not accurate enough, not an absolute standard). One cited comment reports a single game rated **1d in the middlegame, 8d in the endgame and 6d in the opening**. Another says the displayed percentage is the probability of AI use, not the match rate ([Douyin, snippet only](https://www.douyin.com/video/7619663794666523947)). A Zhihu answer says a game played with phone-AI help scored **9.7d "完全没有嫌疑"** (with no suspicion at all) ([Zhihu, snippet only](https://www.zhihu.com/question/2016652932716732417/answers/updated)). An amateur 6d's explainer asks whether a pro-level score means pro strength ([Bilibili BV11muq6MET7](https://www.bilibili.com/video/BV11muq6MET7/)). Early App Store reviews praise "accurate ratings", but they are few and come from launch week ([App Store CN](https://apps.apple.com/cn/app/%E6%B6%A8%E6%A3%8B%E7%BD%91/id6748336605)). No endorsements, school partnerships or English-language discussion were found. All of this is anecdote. It agrees with the operator's own numbers: single-game values are noisy, and the AI detection gives false negatives.

## Zhangqi is the only one of these that turns a single game into a dan rank

The same two primitives, agreement with the engine and per-move loss, underlie every system below. Only Zhangqi turns one game into a calibrated rank.

| System | Agreement metric | Loss / quality | Rank from moves? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Zhangqi | first-choice rate + "good move" rate (≤2 pts, ≤5 % wr, >5 % visits) | mean point loss, 6 levels, human-policy 难度 | **Yes**: per game and per phase, 0–12 Fox-anchored, 30-day mean |
| Tencent 绝艺 / Fox | strict top-1 ([Fox news 9715](https://foxwq.com/news/9715.html)) | 稳定性 = mean point loss (snippet only) | No; ranks come from results |
| Golaxy 星阵 | none documented | 推荐度, 子差, 妙手 graded by difficulty, ≤5 问题手 per side, 发挥水准 ([星阵 docs](https://assets.19x19.com/docs/zh/web-%E5%8A%9F%E8%83%BD%E8%AF%B4%E6%98%8E-%E6%8A%A5%E5%91%8A.html)) | No |
| Yike 弈客 鹰眼 | any of a variable 1-to-several candidates; 80 % called "人类极限" (the human limit) ([sina 2019](https://sports.sina.cn/others/2019-05-09/detail-ihvhiews0826200.d.html)) | 评分, 复杂度, 稳定性 (undefined) | No; used as anti-cheat evidence |
| LizzieYzy | top-3 with ≥20 % of top visits; weighted "match value" | mean winrate/score loss, phases at 60/160 | No |
| KaTrain | top-1 and "AI approved" (<0.5 pt, top-5) ([ai.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/sanderland/katrain/master/katrain/core/ai.py)) | complexity-weighted loss, accuracy = 100·0.75^loss | Only its own bots |
| AI Sensei | none | point-loss categories, reportedly rank-dependent ([OGS forum](https://forums.online-go.com/t/move-categorization-similar-to-ai-sensei-chess-com/55449)) | No |

Two things stand out. First, **"吻合度" means something different on every platform**, so numbers cannot be carried across platforms. Yike's 2019 data shows why one game is weak evidence: the per-game standard deviation (~0.10) is about as large as the whole gap between its user games (0.46) and pro and AI games (0.60) ([sina 2019](https://sports.sina.cn/others/2019-05-09/detail-ihvhiews0826200.d.html)). Second, Zhangqi's design converges on the better ideas in the field. Its 难度 is a human-policy error rate. Its filtered problem moves resemble AI Sensei's rank-dependent categories. Its "surprise" score echoes Golaxy's 妙手 graded by difficulty. Its server model replaces match rate with a learned mapping. The research benchmark remains the ICLR 2025 strength estimator, which needs about **15 games for > 80 % rank accuracy** ([Chen et al.](https://rlg.iis.sinica.edu.tw/papers/strength-estimator/)). Zhangqi's single-game claims should be read against that number.

## What GoRanks should take from it

**Zhangqi independently picked GoRanks' difficulty definition, and that is worth saying.** Its 难度, 1 − P(a strong human plays a move within 2 points of the best), is nearly the same as GoRanks' rule that difficulty is P(9d loses ≥ 2 points). The differences are Zhangqi's extra 5 % winrate and 5 % visit-share conditions and its vague "top amateur / professional" profile. GoRanks fixes the profile at 9d, which makes the number reproducible. Zhangqi's unused "surprise" score is in effect GoRanks' Key Move Found % with a continuous difficulty weight. GoRanks could test whether a difficulty-weighted version predicts rank better than the thresholded count, and report that in the benchmark log, without changing the fixed column set. Zhangqi's level-filtered 问题手 is also a cheap Gifu feature: hide mistakes where the player's own rank profile gives the good moves a low probability. GoRanks already computes those probabilities per move.

**Publish what Zhangqi hides.** Zhangqi's weakest point is that its calibration is undocumented. Its own sample shows the top of the scale compressing, and no one has checked its kyu and low-dan range. GoRanks' grouped-by-player cross-validation, with MAE, bias and ±1 per band and era, is precisely the missing artefact. Since GoRanks trains on Fox data as well as OGS and KGS, it can also test Zhangqi's implied offset (a KataGo `rank_5k` profile ≈ Fox 1d) directly. One check is the profile whose likelihood best fits Fox 1d games. Another is the offset between the scales. The result tells Gifu how to label its output for Chinese users. The previous report found that KataGo's profiles are KGS-2020 profiles. Zhangqi's table is the first outside hint of how far Fox sits from that scale, but it measures bot strength, not human likelihood, so it must not be copied.

**Model depth and era instead of warning about them.** Zhangqi gates on visits (> 15 visits per move to send a move, ×10 compute for any estimate, ≥ 4,500 for filters and PDFs), and it admits that shallow analysis can reverse conclusions and that pre-AlphaGo games score low. GoRanks already keeps every visit level and stratifies eras (pre ≤ 2015, early, current), and it has the `preaz_`/`rank_` profile pair. It should report bias per visit level and per era as first-class results. The aim is to show the drift Zhangqi only warns about, and to correct it.

**Aggregate like a posterior, not a 30-day mean.** Zhangqi's rules (≥ 5 games, a ± band until 20 deep reviews, a 30-day window, handicap excluded, AI-like and "not my game" games dropped) are a sensible product shape, and GoRanks' exclusions list and its handicap stratum already cover the last three. But a weighted mean of per-game point estimates throws information away. Summing per-game rank log-likelihoods, using Bradley–Terry style as in the reference method, gives the ± directly from the data. It degrades gracefully with few games and resists the "1d middlegame / 8d endgame" outliers that users complain about. The display can still follow Zhangqi's lead: a value with a ± band, the number of games behind it, and the outlying games listed first.

## Conclusion

Zhangqi shows that a market exists for "you played at Fox 6d in this game", and that the hard part is not the review metrics. Those are a page of thresholds anyone can copy, and its own are partly visible in its JavaScript. The hard part is a calibrated, honest mapping to rank and a defensible statement of its error. Zhangqi keeps that part closed, and the only accuracy evidence it offers shows strong amateurs and pros overlapping within one game. An open estimator that publishes per-band error, models analysis depth and era, and aggregates games with real uncertainty would not just match Zhangqi. It would provide the validation that Zhangqi's own tooltips ("仅供参考", for reference only) admit is missing.
